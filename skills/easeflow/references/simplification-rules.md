# Simplification rules

Use these to design the proposed flow. Each rule names the persona that most needs it. When a change follows a rule, cite it in the report by number (for example, "R3").

## Shape of the flow

**R1. One obvious first action.** The first screen after sign-up has one primary button that starts the core win. Everything else is secondary or hidden. (Harold, Priya)
> ChatGPT model: a single box that says what to do. No dashboard, no menu tour.

**R2. Value before configuration.** Show a result, preview or sample before asking for setup. Settings come after the user has seen why they matter. (Priya, Harold)

**R3. Ask only what's needed now.** Each step asks only for what the very next action needs. Defer everything else to later or make it optional. (Ramesh, Harold)

**R4. Smart defaults over choices.** Replace decisions with a recommended default the user can change later. A pre-selected option and "Continue" beats three equal options. (Harold, Literal Follower)

**R5. Progressive disclosure.** Advanced features live behind "Advanced", "More options", or appear in context when relevant. They're never deleted, just moved. (Harold, Alex)

**R6. One task per screen.** If a screen has two jobs, split it, or cut one. (Literal Follower, Margaret)

## Guided onboarding

**R7. Onboarding does real work.** 3–5 steps that set the product up (pick the goal, add the first contact, approve the first draft), not a tour of features. (Priya, Harold)

**R8. Always skippable, always resumable.** One click to skip; progress saved after every step; "Continue where you left off" on return. Shown once. (Alex, Sam)

**R9. Show progress.** "Step 2 of 4" and a clear finish line. (Grace, Sam)

## Words

**R10. Plain words, literal verbs.** Buttons say exactly what happens: "Send email", "Save and continue", "Add contact". No "Submit", "Go", "Let's roll". (Literal Follower, Ana)

**R11. Rename jargon to the user's goal.** "ICP" becomes "Who do you want to reach?" "Sequence" becomes "Follow-up emails". "Workspace" becomes your company name. (Harold, Ana)

**R12. Say what happens next.** After every action, confirm what was done and what comes next: "Saved. Next: choose who to send to." (Grace, Literal Follower)

**R13. Kind, fixable errors.** Say what's wrong in plain words, and exactly how to fix it, next to the field. State formats before the error, not after. Never show raw codes. (Grace, Literal Follower)

## Trust

**R14. Explain every commitment in one sentence.** Payment, permissions and anything sent on the user's behalf get a plain reason, a preview and a way back. (Frank, Grace)

**R15. Preview before send; undo after.** Nothing irreversible happens without a preview or confirmation. Offer undo where possible. (Frank, Grace)

**R16. Price in plain numbers, before the card.** (Frank, Ramesh)

## Layout and access

**R17. Big, labelled, high-contrast.** Primary buttons are large, labelled with text (not icon only), and at least 4.5:1 contrast. Base text at least 16px. (Margaret, Harold)

**R18. Works on a phone and at 200% zoom.** No horizontal scroll, no hover-only actions, tap targets at least 44px. (Jay, Margaret)

**R19. Calm navigation.** At most 5 top-level navigation items on the first screen. Hide sections that are empty or irrelevant until the user has something there. (Harold)

**R20. Empty states lead somewhere.** Every empty screen says what will appear here and has one button to make it appear. (Literal Follower)

## Hand-off

**R21. Built for someone else to set up.** Where buyers delegate, allow setting up on someone's behalf and an invite that lands straight on the core win. (Linda)

## Guardrails

- Never remove a capability without listing it under "Recommend removing" with a reason.
- Never trade away accessibility for simplicity.
- If a simplification needs a product decision (pricing, data policy, removing a feature), flag it as **Needs your decision** rather than assuming.
