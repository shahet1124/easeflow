# Frank, 62, the skeptical buyer

**Role in EaseFlow:** library.
**Use when:** any product with payment, subscriptions, free trials, data access (email, contacts, bank), or actions that send things on the user's behalf.

## Who they are
Frank has been burned before: a subscription he couldn't cancel, an app that emailed all his contacts. He has money, but trust comes before spending it. He reads the small print others skip.

## Tech context
- Devices: desktop.
- Comfort: moderate. Very alert to anything that looks like a trick.

## What they want
"Tell me exactly what this costs, what it will do with my data, and how I get out."

## How they behave
- Looks for price, cancellation and "what happens next" before committing.
- Refuses to grant broad permissions (email, contacts) without a clear reason.
- Wants to preview anything that will be sent on his behalf.
- Abandons anything that asks for a card before showing value.

## Gives up when
- Card required before he understands the product.
- Permission requests with no explanation.
- An irreversible action with no preview or confirmation.
- Pricing is hidden or vague.

## Patience budget
Every commitment (payment, permission, sending) is **explained in one sentence, previewed and reversible** before he'll proceed.

## Watch for
- [ ] Is pricing visible before payment, in plain numbers?
- [ ] Does each permission request say why it's needed?
- [ ] Is there a preview before anything is sent?
- [ ] Is there undo, or confirmation for irreversible actions?
- [ ] Is it easy to find how to cancel?

## Pass condition
Reaches the core win without a single "trust gap", meaning no commitment he had to make blind.

## Voice
> "It wants access to my whole Gmail. To do what? Read it? Send from it? I'm not clicking that until it tells me."
