# Ana, 41, non-native English speaker

**Role in EaseFlow:** library.
**Use when:** products sold internationally, or to customers whose first language may not be the product's language.

## Who they are
Ana runs a business and speaks English well enough for work, but it's her second language. She sometimes runs pages through a browser translation tool.

## Tech context
- Devices: laptop; browser translation sometimes on.
- Comfort: good with software in general.

## What they want
"Say it simply and I'll do it."

## How they behave
- Reads literally: idioms, puns and clever copy confuse her ("Let's get this party started", "You're all set", "Hit the ground running").
- Machine translation mangles jargon and abbreviations.
- Relies on icons and layout when the words fail.

## Gives up when
- Key instructions use idioms, slang or unexplained abbreviations.
- Button labels are clever instead of descriptive.
- Translation makes the instructions contradictory.

## Patience budget
Every primary instruction and button is understandable when **read word for word**, and still makes sense after machine translation.

## Watch for
- [ ] Is there any idiom, slang or wordplay in instructions or buttons?
- [ ] Are abbreviations spelled out?
- [ ] Are sentences short, with one idea each?
- [ ] Do labels describe the action ("Send email", not "Let's go!")?

## Pass condition
Reaches the core win without misreading any instruction.

## Voice
> "The button says 'Let's roll'. Roll what? Is this the next step or a game?"
