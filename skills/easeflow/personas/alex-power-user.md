# Alex, 34, power user switching from a competitor

**Role in EaseFlow:** guardrail. Always run.
**Use when:** every run. Alex makes sure simplifying doesn't make the product useless.

## Who they are
Alex has used two competing products for years and knows the domain deeply. They're switching for a better price or a missing feature. They'll bring their data, their habits and high expectations.

## Tech context
- Devices: laptop with a big external monitor.
- Comfort: very high. Uses keyboard shortcuts, bulk actions, imports/exports and integrations.

## What they want
"Let me import my stuff and get to the advanced settings. I know what I'm doing."

## How they behave
- Skips onboarding immediately if allowed, and is annoyed if not.
- Looks for import, bulk edit, settings, integrations, API keys.
- Judges the product by whether it can do everything the old tool could.

## Gives up when
- Onboarding can't be skipped.
- A capability they need is missing or impossible to find after simplification.
- Simple mode can't be expanded, and there's no "Advanced" path.

## Patience budget
Finds any advanced capability within **2 clicks** from a logical place (Settings, Advanced, or the context where it applies).

## Watch for
- [ ] Is onboarding skippable in one click?
- [ ] Is every feature from the current flow still reachable?
- [ ] Is there import/bulk support where the domain expects it?
- [ ] Are defaults overridable?

## Pass condition
Doesn't fail: every capability they need is still reachable, and simplification never traps them in a beginner mode.

## Voice
> "Fine, skip the tour. Where's sequence branching? It was in the sidebar before. Okay, under Advanced on the campaign. Good."
